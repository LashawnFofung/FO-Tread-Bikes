## 1. Executive Summary

FO Tread Bikes launched in a competitive bicycle market with one differentiator: **3D-printed carbon fiber technology** for lighter, stronger, more affordable bikes. Over six quarters, the company:

- **Grew revenue 9×,** from $0.51M in Q2 to **$4.66M** in Q6 ($14.1M in total)
- **Reached profitability in Q5,** as planned, with **$1.09M** in net income
- **Led the market twice:** #1 in market share at launch (41%) and again in Q5
- **Earned the industry's top Balanced Scorecard** in Q2 and Q4, and finished with a cumulative score of **7.77 vs. a 5.84 industry average**
- **Carried no debt,** keeping a perfect 1.000 financial risk score

**The main shortfall:** production capacity didn't keep up with demand. In Q5–Q6, **7,119 customer orders went unfilled**, which slowed revenue growth to 5% in Q6 and pushed market performance to the industry's lowest.

## 5. Financial Performance

| | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 | Q6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | — | $0.51M | $1.01M | $3.52M | $4.42M | $4.66M |
| **Gross margin** | — | 56.5% | 67.9% | 66.5% | 64.1% | 62.5% |
| **R&D** | $0.15M | — | $0.09M | $1.91M | $0.44M | $2.36M |
| **Net income** | −$0.35M | −$0.14M | −$0.03M | −$0.98M | **+$1.09M** | −$1.03M |
| **Profit before R&D** | −$0.20M | −$0.14M | +$0.06M | +$0.93M | +$1.53M | +$1.33M |
| **Ending cash** | $0.37M | $0.81M | $0.59M | $1.93M | $3.10M | $1.90M |

**Findings**
- **Revenue grew 9× with healthy margins.** Gross margin stayed between 62% and 68% from Q3 on.
- **The core business was profitable from Q3.** Adding R&D back to net income shows positive operating results every quarter from Q3. The Q4 and Q6 net losses came from large R&D investments in new technology and models.
- **Funding was conservative.** $5.0M in equity, no loans. By Q6, equity stood at $3.57M after cumulative net losses of $1.43M.

## 6. Market Performance

| | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 | Q6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FO Tread share** | **41.4%** | 17.9% | 27.7% | **25.1%** | 21.0% |
| **Rank (of 5)** | **#1** | #4 | #2 | **#1** | #3 |
| **FO Tread demand (units)** | 570 | 659 | 2,677 | 5,608 | 8,443 |

**Findings**
- **A dominant launch.** FO Tread led all three segments in Q2, including 58% of Mountain and 50% of Speed.
- **Competitors caught up quickly in Q3,** cutting share by more than half.
- **Recovery to #1 in Q5,** supported by new models and Best Brand awards.
- **The market outgrew FO Tread.** Total demand rose 4.2× from Q4 to Q6, while FO Tread's rose 3.2×, led by BIK3D's 19× surge.

## 7. Operations

| | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 | Q6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Units produced** | 387 | 659 | 2,637 | 3,371 | 3,561 |
| **Demand** | 570 | 659 | 2,677 | 5,608 | 8,443 |
| **Lost sales** | 183 | 0 | 40 | **2,237** | **4,882** |
| **Demand served** | 68% | 100% | 99% | 60% | **42%** |
| **Worker productivity** | 58.8% | 70.0% | 76.7% | 83.8% | 88.5% |

**Findings**
- **Productivity improved every quarter,** up 30 points, earning the Best Production Workers trophy in Q5.
- **The plant ran at 100% capacity with overtime from Q4 on,** adding about $7.50 per bike in labor cost.
- **No capacity was added in Q5,** so planned capacity stayed at 3,575 units in Q6 while demand rose 51%.
- **Result:** 7,119 lost sales in Q5–Q6. In Q6, FO Tread filled fewer than half of its orders.

## 8. Balanced Scorecard

The simulation's **total performance** score multiplies ten indicators, so a weakness in any one area pulls down the whole.

| | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 | Q6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FO Tread** | **1.28** 🥇 | 1.71 | **14.33** 🥇 | 9.75 | 12.49 |
| **Industry average** | 0.37 | 1.37 | 4.01 | 11.08 | 16.65 |

🥇 = highest in the industry that quarter

**Cumulative through Q6**

| Strengths (🥇 industry best) | Score | | Areas to improve | Score | Industry avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Financial performance | 39.006 | | Market performance | 0.164 | 0.167 |
| Investment in future | 6.385 | | Marketing effectiveness | 0.738 (lowest) | 0.757 |
| Asset management | 0.912 | | Wealth | 0.713 (lowest) | 0.963 |
| Manufacturing productivity | 0.706 | | | | |
| Financial risk | 1.000 (perfect) | | | | |

**Total performance:** 7.770, above the 5.840 industry average

**Interpretation:** FO Tread led the industry through Q4, ranking first in 6 of 10 indicators that quarter. In Q5–Q6, stock-outs lowered market performance (which multiplies share by the percent of demand served), and the Q6 R&D loss lowered wealth. Both dragged the total below average.

## 10. Plan vs. Actual

The earlier version of this report included several figures that don't match the simulation's final financial statements. They appear to come from business plan projections. Both are shown here for transparency; the rest of this report uses the financial statements.

| Measure (Q6) | Earlier report / plan | Financial statements |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | 3,630 units (projected) | **8,443 units** |
| Revenue | $5.36M (projected) | **$4.66M** |
| Total assets | $4.87M | **$3.57M** |
| Total equity | $4.32M | **$3.57M** |
| Cash | $1.50M | **$1.90M** |
| Operating cash flow | +$0.28M | **−$0.96M** |
| ROI on initial $1.5M investment | 127.68% (projected) | Not supported: $5.0M invested in total; equity of $3.57M at Q6 |

**What the gap shows:** demand came in at **more than twice the plan**, but revenue fell short of plan because production couldn't fill the extra orders. The forecast underestimated demand by more than half, which helps explain why capacity fell short.

## 11. Key Learnings

| Learning | Evidence | What I'd do next time |
|---|---|---|
| **Capacity is a strategic decision, not just an operational one** | 7,119 lost sales in Q5–Q6; $0 capacity investment in Q5 | Forecast demand with a growth buffer and invest ahead of it, using profits like Q5's $1.09M |
| **Excess capacity costs less than lost sales** | Q3's excess capacity cost $30K; Q6 lost 4,882 sales | Accept some idle capacity to avoid stock-outs |
| **Balance matters in a multiplied score** | Industry-best financial and operating scores, but low market performance pulled the total below average | Watch the weakest indicator, not just the strongest |
| **Marketing spend needs measurement** | Marketing effectiveness was the industry's lowest cumulatively | Track brand and ad results by segment and shift spend to what works |
| **Innovation pays, but timing matters** | R&D built the brand and new models, but large R&D quarters (Q4, Q6) caused net losses | Pace R&D alongside capacity so new demand can be served |
