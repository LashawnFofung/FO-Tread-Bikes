## The Simulation

The WGU MBA capstone runs on [Marketplace Simulations](https://www.marketplace-simulation.com). Each quarter, teams make decisions across the whole business — product design, pricing, manufacturing, marketing, sales channels, HR, and finance — and compete for the same customers.

FO Tread Bikes competed in three segments: **Recreation**, **Speed**, and **Mountain**, applying **Conscious Capitalism** principles: create value for customers, employees, investors, and the community, not just profit.

## Strategy

| Pillar | What we did |
|---|---|
| **🔬 Innovation** | Pioneered 3D-printed carbon fiber parts for stronger, lighter, cheaper bikes. Launched Mountain 3 (Q4), Mountain 3D (Q5), and CF Vector 3, AERO 3, and Mountain 4 (Q6). |
| **🌍 Global reach** | Opened stores in New York City (flagship), Amsterdam, Bangalore, and Rio de Janeiro |
| **🏭 Operations** | Expanded fixed capacity and improved productivity to lower unit costs |
| **📣 Marketing** | Increased advertising, internet marketing, and major ad placements |
| **👥 People** | Invested in pay, benefits, and training to reduce turnover and raise productivity |
| **💰 Finance** | Funded growth with equity, not debt, and reinvested heavily in R&D |

## Results

*Figures from the simulation's financial statements in [`Data/`](Data).*

| | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 | Q6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | — | $0.51M | $1.01M | $3.52M | $4.42M | **$4.66M** |
| **Gross profit** | — | $0.29M | $0.69M | $2.34M | $2.83M | $2.92M |
| **R&D spend** | $0.15M | — | $0.09M | $1.91M | $0.44M | $2.36M |
| **Net income** | −$0.35M | −$0.14M | −$0.03M | −$0.98M | **+$1.09M** | −$1.03M |
| **Ending cash** | $0.37M | $0.81M | $0.59M | $1.93M | $3.10M | $1.90M |

**Reading the numbers:**
- **Revenue grew about 9×** from the first selling quarter to the last.
- **Gross margin stayed strong** at roughly 63% in Q6.
- **Q5 was profitable.** The **Q6 loss came largely from $2.36M in R&D**, in the quarter three new 3D-printed models launched.
- **No debt in any quarter,** which kept financial risk at a perfect score.

## What I'd Do Differently

| Issue | Evidence | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Production couldn't keep up** | Shortfall of **2,237 units in Q5** and **4,882 in Q6** | Invest in capacity earlier, sized to demand forecasts, not current sales |
| **Market share slipped** | Share fell from **41% in Q2 to 18% in Q3**; market performance stayed below average from Q4 | Track share every quarter and respond faster when competitors move |
| **Marketing return lagged** | Marketing effectiveness ended near the bottom of the range | Measure ad spend by segment and shift budget to what works |
