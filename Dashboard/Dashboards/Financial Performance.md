## Profit Before R&D

Because R&D is expensed in the quarter it's spent, big R&D quarters show losses even when the core business is profitable. Adding R&D back shows how the underlying operations performed:

| | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 | Q6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Net income** | −$0.35M | −$0.14M | −$0.03M | −$0.98M | $1.09M | −$1.03M |
| **+ R&D** | $0.15M | — | $0.09M | $1.91M | $0.44M | $2.36M |
| **= Profit before R&D** | −$0.20M | −$0.14M | **+$0.06M** | **+$0.93M** | **+$1.53M** | **+$1.33M** |

**From Q3 on, the core business was profitable every quarter.** The Q4 and Q6 losses reflect investment in future products, not weak operations.

## What the Numbers Show

**Strengths**
- **Fast, efficient growth:** revenue grew 9× while gross margin stayed above 62% from Q3 on.
- **A profitable core business:** profit before R&D was positive every quarter from Q3.
- **Disciplined funding:** equity only, no debt.

**Watch-outs**
- **Revenue growth stalled at 5% in Q6** because production capacity couldn't keep up with demand. With $1.90M in cash at the end of Q6 and capacity investment of only $0.24M that quarter, more capacity investment could have captured more of the 8,443 units customers wanted.
- **Two large R&D bets (Q4 and Q6) created net losses.** Cumulative net income through Q6 was −$1.43M, which lowered the wealth score.

**Metrics used**

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|

| Gross margin | Gross profit ÷ revenue |
| Profit before R&D | Net income + R&D expense |
| R&D intensity | R&D expense ÷ revenue |
