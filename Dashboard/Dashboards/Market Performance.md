## Demand vs. Sales

Winning demand only counts if you can deliver it.

| Quarter | FO Tread demand | Units sold | Lost sales (stock-outs) | Demand served |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q2 | 570 | 387 | 183 | 68% |
| Q3 | 659 | 659 | 0 | 100% |
| Q4 | 2,677 | 2,637 | 40 | 99% |
| Q5 | 5,608 | 3,371 | **2,237** | 60% |
| Q6 | 8,443 | 3,561 | **4,882** | **42%** |

In Q5, FO Tread led the market in demand but could deliver only 60% of it. In Q6, more than half of customer demand went unmet. See [Operational Metrics](Operational%20Metrics.md) for the capacity story.

## What the Numbers Show

**What worked**
- **A strong launch.** Competing in all three segments from day one made FO Tread the clear leader in Q2.
- **Brand and product drew customers.** Demand grew from 570 units in Q2 to 8,443 in Q6, and FO Tread was the market leader again in Q5, supported by Best Brand awards in Q3–Q5.

**What held share back**
- **Competitors caught up fast in Q3,** cutting FO Tread's share by more than half.
- **The market grew faster than FO Tread.** Total demand rose 4.2× from Q4 to Q6, while FO Tread's rose 3.2×.
- **Stock-outs wasted demand.** Thousands of customers who wanted an FO Tread bike in Q5–Q6 couldn't get one. The simulation's market performance score multiplies share by the percent of demand served, so this pushed FO Tread's market performance to the industry's lowest in Q5–Q6 (see [Balanced Scorecard](Balanced%20Scorecard.md)).

**The lesson:** marketing and product created the demand. Production capacity was the limit.
