## How the Score Works

The simulation measures how well a team runs the **whole** business, not just profit.

**Total performance = the product of ten indicators:**
financial performance × market performance × marketing effectiveness × investment in future × wealth × HR management × asset management × manufacturing productivity × financial risk × reputation

Because the indicators are **multiplied**, a weakness in any one area pulls down the total. A strong financial score can't make up for poor market performance.

## What the Scorecard Shows

**What went well**
- **Financial performance led the industry for four straight quarters (Q2–Q5).** The business turned sales into profit efficiently.
- **Assets were put to work.** Asset management was the industry's best in four of five quarters, reaching 1.26 in Q6 (sales above the value of assets).
- **People and reputation improved every quarter.** HR management rose from the industry's lowest (0.570) to above average (0.894). Reputation climbed from lowest to best in Q4.
- **No debt, ever.** Financial risk stayed at a perfect 1.000.

**What held the score back**
- **Unmet demand hurt market performance.** The simulation scores market performance as market share × the percent of demand actually served. When production fell 2,237 units short in Q5 and 4,882 in Q6, this indicator dropped to the industry's lowest. Because the total multiplies every indicator, that pulled the overall score below average.
- **Marketing effectiveness lagged.** It rose to 0.833 by Q6 but stayed below the industry average in the final two quarters, and was the lowest cumulatively.
- **Wealth fell in Q6.** The Q6 net loss, largely from $2.36M in R&D, lowered wealth to the industry's lowest that quarter.

**The lesson:** the scorecard rewards balance. FO Tread's financial and operational strengths were real, but under-investing in production capacity created a single weak link that dragged down the whole score.

## Indicator Definitions

*From the simulation's data dictionary.*

| Indicator | What it measures | Formula | Good score |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Financial Performance** | Profit per share from current operations | (Net profit from current operations + gross profit) ÷ 2 ÷ total shares | Higher is better |
| **Market Performance** | Ability to create and serve demand in target segments | Average share in target segments × percent of demand served | Depends on number of competitors |
| **Marketing Effectiveness** | Customer view of brands and ads | (Brand judgment + ad judgment) ÷ 2 | > 0.80 |
| **Investment in Future** | Spending today on future opportunities | (Cumulative future-focused expenses ÷ cumulative net revenue) × 10 + 1 | > 3.0 |
| **Wealth** | Value added to stockholders' investment | Net equity ÷ total stockholder investment | > 1.0 means wealth is being created |
| **Human Resource Management** | Employee pay satisfaction, retention, and morale | Average of compensation satisfaction, retention, and morale | > 0.80 |
| **Asset Management** | Sales generated from assets | Asset turnover | ~1.0 |
| **Manufacturing Productivity** | Reliable, efficient production | Reliability × (capacity used − overtime penalty) | ~0.80 |
| **Financial Risk** | Reliance on debt | √(total equity ÷ total capital) | 1.00 = no debt; < 0.80 is unfavorable |
| **Reputation** | Stakeholder esteem | Stakeholder survey score ÷ 100 | High 0.70s by Q6 |
